# J-REIT market overview

## TL;DR

J-REIT (Japan Real Estate Investment Trust) is the Japan listed-real-estate market layer. It is organized as an investment corporation (投資法人) under the Investment Trust Act rather than as an operating real-estate company. The ARES unitholder survey for February 2026 covered all **58 J-REITs then listed** and reported ¥17.320911 trillion as the surveyed market value of J-REIT investment units held directly by the reported holder categories. “Direct” here means direct ownership of investment units, not ownership of real estate. This survey measure is distinct from the ARES May 2026 market statistics of ¥15.7849 trillion in listed market capitalization and ¥24.4967 trillion in assets under management. The live name count changes with listings, mergers and delistings and should be taken from the [[finance/japan-listed-financial-groups-investable-universe|JPX REIT segment]]. The market launched in September 2001 with Nippon Building Fund and Japan Real Estate as the first two listings.

The defining structural feature is external management by a registered asset-management company. Sponsor ownership, pipeline agreements and unit holdings vary by issuer and must be checked in current disclosures. Use this page as the J-REIT routing surface in [[real-estate-finance/INDEX|real-estate-finance domain]] and follow links into [[real-estate-finance/j-reit-vs-us-reit-governance-comparison|US/JP governance comparison]], [[real-estate-finance/j-reit-sponsor-structure-conflict|sponsor conflict]], [[real-estate-finance/top-10-j-reit-overview-matrix|top 10 matrix]], and [[real-estate-finance/private-reit-japan-vs-listed-j-reit-comparison|private vs listed REIT comparison]] for next-level detail.

J-REIT is not an operating company. The investment corporation holds real estate or real-estate trust beneficiary interests and outsources asset management. The legal title holder depends on the asset-holding structure. This is the structural anchor for the governance and conflict-of-interest questions analyzed in [[real-estate-finance/j-reit-sponsor-structure-conflict|sponsor conflict]] and [[real-estate-finance/j-reit-vs-us-reit-governance-comparison|governance comparison]].

## 2. Market structure

Table evidence (reviewed 2026-07-29): [FSA's J-REIT regulatory overview](https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/refer/legislation/20130416/02.pdf), [JPX's REIT market guide](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/equities/products/reits/guidebook/b5b4pj000003984r-att/REIT.pdf), and issuer securities reports. Trustee, leverage and distribution details are issuer- and period-specific.

| Element | Public surface |
|---|---|
| Issuer entity | Investment corporation that issues investment units (投資口) |
| Operator | External asset-management company; ownership and sponsor / support relationships are issuer-specific |
| Property-level trust trustee | Where a property is held through a trust beneficiary interest, the trustee holds legal title; identify the entity from that property's disclosure rather than treating it as an investment-corporation-wide role |
| Auditor | Listed-company auditor |
| Investment-corporation asset custodian / general administrators | Separate corporation-level appointments disclosed in the securities report or structure page; do not substitute a property's trust trustee or assume all functions use one trust bank |
| Tax treatment | Distribution deductibility requires the statutory conduit conditions; distributing more than 90% of distributable income is one condition, not the whole test |
| Leverage | Bank loans + investment-corporation bonds; LTV is issuer- and period-specific and publicly disclosed |
| Distribution | DPU (distribution per unit) twice a year is the typical pattern |
| Trading | Continuous on JPX, T+2 settlement via JASDEC plumbing (see [[banking/trust-bank-custody-operating-comparison\|trust bank custody operating comparison]]) |

## 5. Why it matters

- J-REIT provides public-market price, valuation, leverage and DPU disclosures; compare coverage and definitions before ranking transparency against other vehicles.
- JPX investor-type data measures trading flow, not issuer ownership. Any link between foreign flow and property cap rates requires matched flow, price and property-market evidence.
- Listed J-REIT IR provides public portfolio, leasing and financing data that can complement listed-developer disclosure in [[finance/INDEX|finance domain]] and [[retail/aeon-group|AEON Group]] / [[retail/seven-and-i-hd|Seven & i HD]] disclosures; coverage and definitions remain issuer-specific.
- Financing instruments vary by issuer and period. Use each debt schedule to connect identified bank loans or investment-corporation bonds to [[banking/INDEX|banking domain]] analysis.
- J-REIT structure provides separate case studies for the investment corporation, external asset manager, sponsor/support relationship, corporation-level asset custodian and any property-level trust trustee; do not collapse those roles.

## 6. Boundary cases

- **J-REIT vs listed developer**: a J-REIT's permitted investments and current scope come from law, its articles, investment policy and portfolio; do not reduce every issuer to an identical “income-producing real estate only” rule. Listed developers such as Mitsui Fudosan, Mitsubishi Estate, Sumitomo Realty, Daiwa House, Tokyu Fudosan, Hulic and Nomura Real Estate (see [[finance/INDEX|finance domain]]) have separately disclosed operating segments.
- **J-REIT vs private REIT (非上場REIT)**: see [[real-estate-finance/private-reit-japan-vs-listed-j-reit-comparison|private vs listed REIT comparison]]. Listing status, investor eligibility, subscription/redemption design and term are product- and fund-specific; “private REIT” does not by itself prove an open-ended pension product.
- **J-REIT vs GK-TK arrangement / private fund**: GK-TK describes a legal and contractual arrangement, not a universal single-asset, single-strategy or closed-end product. Establish asset count, strategy, term, leverage and investor rights from the transaction documents.
- **TSE REIT Index vs TOPIX**: J-REIT investment units are not in TOPIX; they form their own segment with their own TSE REIT Index.
- **DPU yield vs dividend yield**: DPU is distribution per unit, not corporate dividend. The economic distinction matters for taxation and for comparison with [[finance/japan-listed-financial-groups-investable-universe|listed-financial / listed-developer dividend yields]].
