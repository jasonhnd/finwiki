## TL;DR

“Mitsubishi Heavy Export Finance” is not an official legal name or segment name. It is a FinWiki analytical route for reading the interface between large projects and funding providers at Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI, 7011). MHI operates in Energy, Plants & Infrastructure Systems, Aircraft, Defense & Space, and other fields, but public materials do not establish that **every export project uses a standard JBIC + NEXI + megabank structure**. Export-finance and insurance involvement must be determined from transaction-specific contracts, government announcements, and financial-institution releases. For GCAP, GIGO is an intergovernmental organization, while Edgewing is an international JV invested in by BAE Systems, Leonardo, and the Society of Japanese Aerospace Companies (JAIEC); describing MHI as having a direct 3-company JV with BAE / Leonardo is inaccurate.

## 4. Strategy (Energy Transition + Defense + Project Finance)

- **Separate projects from funding providers**: Do not equate MHI's orders and revenue with financing raised by customers, SPVs, governments, or financial institutions.
- **Two-layer GCAP governance**: Separate the government-side GIGO / GCAP Agency from the industry-side Edgewing / national supply chains.
- **Record policy finance from transaction evidence**: Do not infer use in a specific project from general descriptions of JBIC / NEXI programs.
- **Update the consolidation boundary**: Do not treat former Mitsubishi Logisnext as a current MHI consolidated business.

## 5. Regulation and Policy

- As a listed company, it is subject to disclosure under the TSE, the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act, the Companies Act, and IFRS.
- Separate business regulation for defense, space, nuclear power, and export control from financial regulation applicable to banks, insurers, and export-credit agencies.
- For GCAP, prioritize published treaties, national budgets, intergovernmental-organization materials, and international contracts.
