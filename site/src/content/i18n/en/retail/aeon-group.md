# AEON Group retail-finance operating map

## Scope

AEON is a retail group with an integrated financial-services business spanning cards, banking, insurance distribution, mobile payment, and electronic money. This page maps the documented entities and product layers. It does not assume that every AEON-branded service is operated by the same legal entity or that retail data can move across the group without an applicable notice and legal basis.

## Documented group structure

Sources for the following table: AEON's [group-business directory](https://www.aeon.info/company/group/), [AEON Financial Service overview](https://www.aeonfinancial.co.jp/en/corp/about/), and [AEON Bank company outline](https://www.aeonbank.co.jp/sp/company/about/outline/).

| Layer | Documented role | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| AEON Co., Ltd. | Pure holding company managing retail, developer, financial, service, and related businesses | Group parent status does not identify the contracting entity for a financial product |
| AEON Financial Service Co., Ltd. | Listed company responsible for AEON Group's financial-services business | It is a business company, not merely a passive bank holding company |
| AEON Bank, Ltd. | Licensed bank founded in 2006 and opened as a retail bank in 2007 | Deposit, lending, ATM, and investment-product functions sit within banking permissions |
| AEON Insurance Service Co., Ltd. | Insurance agency operating shops primarily in AEON shopping centers | Agency distribution is distinct from underwriting |
| AEON Card | Credit-card product family within the financial-services business | Issuer and terms must be checked on the applicable card agreement |
| WAON / WAON POINT / AEON Pay | Payment and loyalty layers used with AEON's retail channels | Electronic money, points, and app payment have different contractual and regulatory treatment |

AEON Financial Service describes itself as a comprehensive financial group rooted in retail and operating in Japan and ten other Asian countries and regions. Geographic presence alone does not imply identical products or permissions in each market.

## Retail-to-finance distribution

AEON's official operating pages describe bank branches and insurance shops in shopping centers, cards connected to group benefits, ATMs at AEON and other locations, and payment products used during shopping.

Sources for the following table: [AEON Financial Service domestic operations](https://www.aeonfinancial.co.jp/en/corp/enterprise/) and the [AEON Financial Service corporate overview](https://www.aeonfinancial.co.jp/en/corp/about/).

| Retail touchpoint | Financial function documented by AEON | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Shopping-center branch | Deposits, loans, asset-management consultation, and other bank services | Is the customer contracting with AEON Bank or an agent? |
| Insurance shop | Insurance solicitation and comparison through an agency | Which insurer underwrites the selected policy? |
| AEON Card at group stores | Payment plus store-specific benefits | Which products, stores, dates, and exclusions apply? |
| AEON Bank ATM | Cash services, partner-card access, and WAON top-up | Which transaction fee and service hours apply to the card used? |
| WAON / AEON Pay | Stored-value or app-based payment at participating merchants | Which entity issues the value and which terms govern refunds or expiry? |

The documented distribution advantage is access to customers during ordinary shopping. Any claim about lower acquisition cost, better credit outcomes, or cross-product conversion still needs dated management disclosure or measured data.

## Permission map

AEON Financial Service publishes several permissions and registrations in its corporate profile, including bank agency, credit-card-number handling, third-party prepaid payment instruments, and electronic payment intermediary activity.

Sources for the following table: [AEON Financial Service permissions and registrations](https://www.aeonfinancial.co.jp/en/corp/about/) and [AEON Bank's company information](https://www.aeonbank.co.jp/company/).

| Activity | Publicly identified legal layer | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Deposits and bank loans | AEON Bank | Bank products are not provided merely under a retailer's general corporate authority |
| Bank intermediation | AEON Financial Service as bank agent for AEON Bank | Agency permission is not a separate banking license |
| Credit-card activity | Relevant card issuer and registered card-number-handling entity | Product agreement identifies the issuer and card terms |
| Third-party prepaid value | Registered prepaid-payment-instrument issuer | Safeguarding and redemption rules differ from bank deposits |
| Insurance sales | Insurance agency | The underwriting insurer bears the policy risk |
| Overseas consumer finance | Local subsidiaries and local permissions | Japanese registrations do not passport automatically across Asia |

## What the AEON case supports

- Retail stores and shopping centers can act as recurring distribution points for regulated financial services.
- Card, bank, prepaid, loyalty, insurance, and app-payment functions remain legally distinct even when presented under one group brand.
- AEON Bank's branch and product design is tied to shopping-center access, while AEON Financial Service also operates beyond Japan through local entities.
- Any comparison with Seven Bank or Lawson Bank should distinguish a retail full-service bank model from an ATM-platform model.

## Comparison checklist

Sources for the following checklist table: the AEON sources above, [Seven Bank's company overview](https://www.sevenbank.co.jp/english/corp/company/business.html), and [KDDI's Lawson partnership announcement](https://newsroom.kddi.com/english/news/detail/kddi_pr_secret-79.html).

| Dimension | AEON question | Peer-reading caution |
|---|---|---|
| Physical channel | Are branches, counters, ATMs, or only POS acceptance present? | A convenience-store ATM network is not equivalent to a mall branch network |
| Bank role | Are deposits and loans central or is the bank primarily an ATM operator? | “Owns a bank” is too coarse |
| Payment ownership | Who issues the card, prepaid value, and app payment? | Brand presence at POS does not prove group ownership |
| Loyalty | Is the point closed, partner-based, or transferable? | Point economics require the actual terms |
| Overseas scope | Which subsidiary and regulator cover each market? | Group footprint is not a single cross-border license |
