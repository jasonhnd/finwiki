# Hitachi Capital (former name; now integrated into Mitsubishi HC Capital)

## TL;DR

**Hitachi Capital (日立キャピタル)** was established in 1957 as a leasing and finance company affiliated with Hitachi, Ltd. For many years, it provided the Hitachi group's leasing and vendor-finance functions while building a distinctive global captive-leasing presence in the UK, the US, Europe, and Southeast Asia. **On 2021-04-01, it completed a business integration with Mitsubishi UFJ Lease → creating Mitsubishi HC Capital Inc. (三菱HCキャピタル株式会社, TSE PRIME 8593), and Hitachi Capital ceased to exist as an independent corporate entity.** This page is therefore a **historical-entity + naming-integration note**. For current business / financial information, refer to [[leasing-firms/mitsubishi-hc-capital|Mitsubishi HC Capital]].

## 2. Merger history & business lines (former)

Starting from vendor leasing for the Hitachi group, the former Hitachi Capital differentiated itself from other Japanese leasing companies through global captive-leasing operations in the **UK (Hitachi Capital UK), the US, Europe, and Southeast Asia**. In the **UK** in particular, it was a rare Japanese financial company with a significant local market share in auto leasing, housing finance, and consumer credit.

Principal former business areas:

- Vendor leasing for Hitachi, Ltd. products (IT, industrial equipment, medical equipment)
- General equipment and industrial equipment leasing
- UK captive finance (Hitachi Capital UK = auto leasing + consumer finance + housing loans)
- Global leasing platform across Europe, Southeast Asia, and the Americas
- Auto leasing (domestic corporate vehicles)
- Real estate-related leasing
- Environmental and renewable-energy financing

### 2021-04 Background to the merger with Mitsubishi UFJ Lease

- The former Mitsubishi UFJ Lease ([[megabanks/mufg|MUFG]] group) and the former Hitachi Capital announced their business integration in 2020-09
- The integration was completed on 2021-04-01 → Mitsubishi HC Capital (Mitsubishi HC Capital Inc.) was launched
- The integration gave the company the scale to become one of the three major leasing firms, establishing a three-firm structure of [[leasing-firms/orix-corp|ORIX]] / Mitsubishi HC Capital / [[leasing-firms/tokyo-century|Tokyo Century]]
- The combination of Hitachi's **global captive operations (especially in the UK)** + Mitsubishi UFJ's **major domestic financial platform** provided the strategic synergy
- Hitachi, Ltd. remained a strategic shareholder

## 3. Strategic significance of this transition

The integration of the former Hitachi Capital was one of the largest events in the M&A history of the leasing industry. While upper-mid-tier corporate-affiliated captive leasing companies such as [[leasing-firms/fuyo-lease|Fuyo General Lease]], [[leasing-firms/ricoh-leasing|Ricoh Leasing]], and [[leasing-firms/nec-leasing|NEC Capital Solutions]] maintained their independence, Hitachi Capital chose a large-scale integration.

This resulted from an alignment between [[megabanks/mufg|MUFG]]'s non-bank strategy (consolidating leasing functions within the Mitsubishi group) and Hitachi, Ltd.'s group strategy (making its financial-services business more efficient and expanding it globally).

In the context of [[finance/japan-acquisition-finance|acquisition finance in Japan]], the former Hitachi Capital's global network, especially in the UK, was inherited by Mitsubishi HC Capital and continues to be used in cross-border M&A finance.
